# Proofline Design Standard

Proofline must be judged against mature reputation SaaS products, not hobby AI tools.

## Quality Bar

- The public site must feel trustworthy enough for a local business owner to connect Google Business data.
- The workspace must feel like a real operating product, not a slide deck.
- The UI should be calm, confident, and commercially polished.
- Claims must be paired with visible product surfaces.
- Buttons should show state changes, drawers, confirmations, or audit feedback.

## Visual Principles

- No strategic/internal language on customer-facing pages.
- No broad competitor callouts in customer-facing copy.
- No decorative clutter that makes the product feel like a template.
- Use dense but readable operational layouts for the workspace.
- Keep cards purposeful: actions, evidence, metrics, rules, or outputs.
- Mobile workspace should prioritize current work over long navigation.

## Product Proof

Every major feature should show at least one concrete artifact:

- Review request: message preview and send state.
- AI reply: original review, draft, risk label, approval state.
- Recovery: call script, gift policy, root cause, audit trail.
- Proof library: widget/social/site output.
- Referral: link, lead, booking, reward state.
- Repeat: service cadence, last visit, next reminder.
- Insights: evidence, trend, next action.
- Compliance: rule decision and audit log.

## Acceptance Questions

1. Would an owner trust this with their Google reputation?
2. Does it feel at least as credible as a funded SaaS company?
3. Can the user see what the product actually does?
4. Does each recommendation cite evidence or rules?
5. Does every risky action require approval?
